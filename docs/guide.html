User guide

Plan a full month of shifts from a handful of rules

RailOPS replaces the hand-filled roster. Record each person's shift and rest days once; the month is generated, every shift is counted, gaps are flagged, rotation is balanced, and the Excel sheet you already circulate is produced on demand.

What it does

Replacing the hand-filled grid

Today you fill roughly 660 cells by hand every month — 22 people across 30 days. Every one of those cells follows a pattern: a person's shift, their fixed pair of rest days, occasionally a mid-month shift change, and occasionally leave.

RailOPS stores that pattern instead of the cells. You describe each person once, and the month is generated. When you open a month you have never touched, it is already complete. When someone's circumstances change, you change their rule and the grid updates immediately.

The headcount rows at the bottom of your sheet — Engineers Count and Total Head Counts — are recomputed live, and any day that falls below the minimum you set is highlighted before you send anything out.

How a cell is decided

For every person and every day, the tool checks these in order and uses the first that applies:

1You changed this cell by handkeep your edit
2The date is inside a leave blockLV / FLRT
3The weekday is one of their rest days–
4A rotation range covers the daythat shift
5Otherwisedefault shift

Because a hand edit sits at the top of that list, regenerating a month never overwrites something you set deliberately. Hand-edited cells carry a small blue dot so you can see which ones they are.

Reading the grid

  • Each cell is coloured by its shift: M E N GS P. Days off show as a dim dot. Leave and FLRT are hatched LV so they never read as a shift.
  • Weekend columns are tinted. Today's column is marked with a line.
  • A red border on a cell means a problem with that person on that day. A red bar under a day number means that day is short-staffed.
  • The bottom rows are your Engineers Count / Total Head Counts, recalculated on every change. A number turns red when it is below the minimum for that shift. They sit in their own space below the grid, so they never cover a person's row.

First-time setup

About fifteen minutes, once

  1. Open the tool.

    It loads with your Line 5 September 2026 roster already in place, read from your Excel file. Every person's shift, rest days, rotation and leave has been worked out from the grid.

  2. Go to People and check each person.

    Click a row. Confirm the default shift and the two rest days. Switch on Fixed for anyone who should never be moved by auto-rotate — General Shift and Project staff, for example.

  3. Go to Setup › Shift codes and set minimum headcounts.

    Enter the fewest people each shift needs on any given day. The tool is currently set to Morning 2, Evening 2, Night 3, taken from the thinnest days in your September sheet. Adjust these to your actual requirement — every coverage warning depends on them.

  4. Add any lines you need.

    Setup › Lines › Add line. Each line keeps its own people and rosters. Line 4 is already created, empty.

  5. Back on the Planner, open Insights.

    Anything the tool considers a problem in the current month is listed there. Your September sheet has one: Night falls to 2 people on the 25th and 26th.

Monthly routine

A few minutes instead of an afternoon

  1. Step forward to the new month.

    Use the › arrow. The month is already generated from everyone's rules. Nothing to fill in.

  2. Run Auto-rotate if shifts should change this month.

    Review who moves and why, then Apply. Skip this if everyone stays where they are.

  3. Check the Insights badge.

    If it shows a number, open the panel. Each item says exactly what is wrong. For a short-staffed day, press Show who can cover — it lists people who are off that day, not on leave, and safe to bring in. Click a name to assign them.

  4. Make any hand adjustments directly on the grid.

    Swap two people, extend a shift, mark a one-off day. See Editing the grid.

  5. Export › Excel workbook.

    You get the same layout as your current file — title, legend, coloured codes, headcount formulas — ready to send to management.

Recording leave

Vacation, training, FLRT, or any status you define

  1. People › click the person.
  2. Under Leave & status, press Add leave.

    A new row appears with a date range and a status code.

  3. Set the dates and the code.

    LV for leave, FLRT for that status, or any status code you add in Setup. Changes save as you type.

  4. Return to the Planner.

    The person's cells for those dates are now hatched. If that leaves a shift short on any day, Insights flags it immediately and offers cover.

Leave is stored as a rule, not as cells. It survives regenerating the month, and it stays on the person's record for reference in future months.

Auto-rotate

Fair shift rotation, decided the same way every time

Rotating people between Morning, Evening and Night by hand is where unfairness creeps in. In your September sheet four engineers worked 22 night shifts each while six worked none. Auto-rotate fixes this in one step.

What it does

  1. Counts each person's night-shift load across the months already in the tool.
  2. Moves people one step around the cycle — Morning → Evening → Night → Morning — heaviest night load first, so nobody stays on nights month after month.

    The slider controls how many people move: all the way left moves only who is needed for coverage; all the way right rotates everyone.

  3. Repairs coverage.

    If any shift on any day would fall below its minimum, it moves the best-fitting person across from a shift that has spare people.

  4. Shows you the result before writing anything.

    Who moves, from which shift to which, how many nights they have worked so far, the thinnest day per shift after the change, and a plain-language reason for each move.

  5. Apply writes it into next month and takes you there.
  • People marked Fixed, and anyone on a non-rotating code such as GS or P, are never moved.
  • The result is deterministic: the same inputs always produce the same roster, so a roster you have signed off will not change under you.
  • The Fairness tab in Insights shows nights per person and how wide the gap is, so you can see the effect over time.

Editing the grid

Three ways to change a cell

Click

In Select mode, click any cell. A small menu opens with every shift code and Off. Click one.

Paint

Click a shift chip in the Brush row. Now click a cell, or click-and-drag across a run of cells, to paint that shift. Press Esc to put the brush down.

Type

Click a cell to focus it, then type M E N G P or -. Arrow keys move between cells.

Every edit is undoable with Ctrl+Z. Hand-edited cells show a blue dot in the corner, and they are preserved when you regenerate the month unless you untick Keep my hand edits in the Generate dialog.

Using Insights

The panel on the right of the Planner — the tool checking your roster for you

You never have to run Insights. Every time anything on the grid changes — a cell painted, leave added, a month generated — the whole month is re-checked in the background and the result appears in the panel straight away.

The badge tells you the state at a glance

Look at the Insights button in the toolbar:

  • Red number — that many errors. Something is wrong and needs fixing before the roster goes out.
  • Amber number — that many warnings. Worth a look, but may be perfectly fine.
  • Clear — nothing to fix.

Hover the button for a one-line explanation. Click it to open the panel, which has four tabs: Issues, Coverage, Hours and Fairness. The Hours tab is described under Hours & overtime.

Tab 1

Issues

Every problem in the month, one line each, most serious first. This is where you fix things.

Tab 2

Coverage

A chart per shift showing people on it each day, plus the shift count for every person. This is where you see the shape of the month.

Tab 3

Fairness

Night shifts worked per person. This is where you see who has carried the load, and why Auto-rotate moves who it moves.

Tab 1 — Issues: finding and fixing a problem

Each line says the day, the shift and what is wrong — for example "Day 25: Night has 2, needs 3." Red items are under Must fix; amber items are under Worth a look.

  1. Click the issue.

    The grid scrolls to that day and lights the column up for a moment, so you see the problem in context. If the issue is about one person, their cell is selected as well.

  2. For a short-staffed day, click "Show who can cover →".

    A short list of names appears. These are people who are off that day, not on leave, and who would not end up with a night shift followed by a morning shift. The person with the lightest month so far is listed first, so the extra work goes to whoever has done the least.

  3. Click "Assign" next to a name.

    That person is put on the shift for that day immediately. The grid updates, the headcount row updates, and if the gap is now closed the issue disappears from the list.

  4. If it says nobody is free,

    the day cannot be covered from the current team. You would need to shorten a leave block, borrow someone from another line, or accept the shortfall for that day.

Anyone you assign this way is a normal hand edit: the cell gets a blue dot, Ctrl+Z undoes it, and it survives regenerating the month.

Tab 2 — Coverage: the shape of the month, and counts per person

One small bar chart for each shift. Each bar is one day; the taller the bar, the more people on that shift that day. A red bar is a day below that shift's minimum — exactly the same days that appear as errors in the Issues tab. Above the charts, four tiles give the month's totals: shift-days worked, rest days, leave days, and days in the month.

Below the charts, Employee Breakdown lists every person with their number of Morning, Evening, Night and other shifts, leave days and days off for the month. Type in the filter box to find someone; click a card to jump to their row on the grid.

Tab 3 — Fairness: who has carried the nights

Every person who rotates is listed with the number of night shifts they have worked, added up across all the months stored in the tool. The bar next to each name shows their share. The spread number at the top is the gap between the person with the most nights and the person with the fewest — the smaller it is, the fairer the roster.

This is the information Auto-rotate reads. When it proposes moving someone off nights, it is because they are near the top of this list. So if you ever wonder why a rotation moved a particular person, this tab is the answer.

In your September roster the spread is 22: four engineers worked 22 night shifts each while six worked none. Running Auto-rotate into October narrows that gap.

Where the rules come from

Every check in Insights depends on numbers you control. The minimum people per day for each shift (Setup › Shift codes) decides what counts as short-staffed. The days in a row limit (Setup › Planner rules) decides when a long run is flagged. Change a number there and every check re-runs instantly. Set a minimum to 0 to switch that check off for that shift.

Hours & overtime

Worked hours and OT, calculated for every person automatically

A standard working day is 9 hours (change it in Setup › Planner rules & working hours). Each shift's hours come from its timing, so Morning 06:00 – 15:00 is 9 hours and Night Late 19:00 – 07:00 is 12. Overtime is worked out on every edit:

  • Working day: any hours past 9 are OT. A 12-hour shift gives 3 hours OT; a 9-hour shift with 2 extra hours gives 2.
  • Extra duty on a rest day: the whole shift is OT. Painting M on someone's rest day adds 9 hours OT.
  • Day off or leave: no extra hours can be added and no OT is counted. To call someone in on a day off, give them a shift — that is an extra duty.

Where to find it

On the grid

The two columns pinned to the right of the Planner, Hrs and OT, show each person's month total. A day with overtime has an amber line under the shift; hover any cell to see its hours.

In Insights › Hours

Month totals, how many people have OT, and a table of days, hours, extra hours and OT per person. Sort by most OT; click a name to jump to their first OT day.

In the Excel export

Every export carries Hours and OT per person. If you imported your own sheet with an OT column, it is refilled from the roster.

In Setup

Each shift code shows its hours next to its timing, and the standard day is set under Planner rules & working hours.

Entering extra hours

  1. Make sure no brush is selected

    (press Esc), then click the person's cell for that day.

  2. Under Extra hours, click +1, +2 or +3,

    or type any amount (half hours allowed) and press Enter. The cell shows a small amber +2, and the OT column updates at once.

  3. To remove them,

    open the cell again and click ✕. Extra hours can be undone with Ctrl+Z and are kept when the month is regenerated.

To give someone an extra duty shift, just paint a shift onto their rest day. The OT is counted for you.

What it checks

The six rules behind the Issues tab

A shift is below its minimum headcount on a dayError
A shift has nobody on it at all on a dayError
Night shift followed by Morning shift the next day — no rest gap between 07:00 and 06:00Error
Someone is rostered on a day inside their own leave blockError
Someone is working on one of their declared rest daysWarning
Someone works more than 6 days in a row (threshold adjustable in Setup)Warning

Errors mean the roster is wrong. Warnings are worth a look but can be legitimate — for instance, someone covering on a rest day by agreement.

Display, save & backups

Answers to the questions raised in review

Zoom in and out

Use the − 100% + control in the Planner toolbar. Each press changes the grid by 10%, from 70% up to 140%. Cells, rows, the name column and the text inside all scale together. Click the percentage to return to 100%. On a small screen the same control is inside the ⋯ menu, and it is also in Setup › Display & Fonts.

Font size and style

Setup › Display & Fonts has two settings. Font size — Compact, Normal or Large — changes text throughout the application. Font family — Inter (default), System or Monospace — changes the typeface. On a wide screen the font-size choice is also a dropdown in the toolbar. Both are remembered on this device.

How to save

Every change you make is saved automatically the moment you make it — painting a cell, adding leave, running Generate. There is nothing to lose by closing the browser. The Save button in the toolbar, with its ✓ Saved / Saving… status, is there so you can see that this is happening and press it if you want to be sure.

Saving keeps the roster inside this browser. To get a file you can send or keep elsewhere, use Export › Excel workbook or Setup › Download backup.

Backups and restore

  1. Setup › Data & Backups › Download backup (JSON).

    Saves one file containing everything — all lines, people, rules, leave and every saved month. Keep it somewhere safe. Do this at the end of each month.

  2. To restore, press Restore from backup and choose that file.

    The tool checks the file is a valid RailOPS backup, replaces what is in the browser with it, and reloads. Use this to move to another computer or to recover after clearing browser data.

Restore needs a backup file to exist first. If you have never pressed Download backup, there is nothing to restore yet.

What "Reset to Demo Data" means

This wipes everything in the browser and reloads the sample September 2026 roster that the tool was delivered with. It is for returning to a clean starting point after testing. It cannot be undone, so download a backup first if there is anything you want to keep.

Excel in and out

Works with the layout you already use

Import an existing file

  1. Planner › Import. Drop your .xlsx file or choose it.
  2. Pick the sheet if the workbook has more than one — for example the August or September tab.
  3. Review what was understood.

    The tool reads the day columns and works backwards: where - falls reveals each person's rest days, where the code changes reveals rotation blocks, and red-filled empty cells become leave. You see a table of every person's shift, rest days and rotation before anything is saved.

  4. Choose the line and press Import.

    By default it replaces the people on that line with the ones in the file. Untick Replace to add instead.

Export

Excel workbook

Your layout: title row, shift-timing legend, weekday and date headers, colour-filled codes, weekday repeat row, and the headcount rows as live COUNTIF formulas that keep recalculating in Excel. Landscape, fit to one page. About one-eighth the size of your current file.

Print / PDF

Opens the browser print dialog on the grid. Choose landscape and "Save as PDF" for a wall sheet.

Full backup

Everything — lines, people, rules, leave and saved months — as one file. Restore from Setup › Data & Backups.

Your data

Where it lives and how to keep it safe

All data is stored inside your browser on your own computer. Nothing is sent to any server and there is no login. This means:

  • It works without an internet connection once the page is open.
  • It is tied to that browser on that machine. To move to another computer, use Setup › Download backup and then Restore from backup on the new one.
  • Clearing the browser's site data will remove it. Take a backup before doing that.

Take a backup at the end of each month. It takes one click and it is the only copy.

Industry benchmark

The standard RailOPS is built to

RailOPS follows the established e-roster model — data-driven shift assignment, AI-assisted scheduling, real-time leave tracking and compliance checks — as set out by commercial systems such as the PinShift eRoster System. The difference is focus: RailOPS is tailored to a fixed three-shift, 5-on / 2-off operation and produces the Excel format your management already uses.
